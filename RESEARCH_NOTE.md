# Research note

## One-paragraph summary

I developed an independent empirical project examining whether online ratings can recover before a product problem is fixed. I assembled and analyzed 38,056 multilingual Steam reviews around a 2024 *Stardew Valley* localization controversy. Using timestamp-based event windows, contemporaneous language comparisons, review-arrival decomposition, robustness checks, and a blinded text audit, I found that the Simplified Chinese-English recommendation gap began narrowing before the localization was reverted. I present this as an observational timing pattern rather than a causal treatment effect.

## Skills demonstrated

- Translating an open-ended platform question into a testable empirical design.
- Cleaning and structuring multilingual, timestamped behavioral data.
- Comparing event stages and equal-duration windows.
- Using alternative comparison groups and placebo periods.
- Separating vote outcomes from written-content evidence.
- Communicating uncertainty and limits to causal interpretation.

## Relevance to future research

The project connects applied econometrics, platform economics, consumer behavior, service recovery, and digital-product decision-making. It is especially relevant to research involving online platforms, customer evaluation, technology adoption, brand-influencer relationships, and large-scale behavioral data.
